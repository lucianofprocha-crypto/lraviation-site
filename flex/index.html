What is FLEX Takeoff? (concept and method)

Reduced Thrust (FLEX) Takeoff is the "assumed temperature method": instead of taking off with the EPR calculated for the actual ambient temperature, a higher (fictitious) temperature is assumed to compute a reduced EPR - sparing the engine - as long as at least 75% of rated takeoff thrust is maintained.

How it works in practice: the same Performance Handbook table (weight x OAT) is used twice. First, the actual temperature (OAT) and weight are entered to obtain the Rated EPR, V1/VR/V2 speeds, and the required field length. Then, the table is "walked forward" into higher-temperature columns (assumed temperature) on the same weight row, up to the highest temperature whose required field length still fits within the available runway length (adjusted for wind, slope, and spoilers). The EPR of that "assumed" column is the FLEX EPR.

Limits: the assumed temperature can never exceed the tabulated MAX TEMP for that weight, and the resulting EPR can never drop below 75% of the (actual) Rated EPR.

This calculator digitizes all 35 takeoff planning tables from the G550 Performance Handbook (Rev. 43, section 01-01-30): dry/wet runway, airport pressure altitude (0 to 8,000 ft), flaps 10°/20°, and engine bleed ON/OFF (where applicable) - reproducing the same result as the printed table, just faster to look up.
