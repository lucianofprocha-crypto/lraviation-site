Units and definitions

Weights in pounds (lb), lengths in inches (in), moments in in·lb. To avoid large numbers, moments are divided by 10,000 (the “Moment/10000” column).

Moment = Weight × Arm (FS)  ·  CG = ΣMoment ÷ ΣWeight
Zero Fuel Weight (ZFW)
Airplane weight with no usable fuel. On the G550 it is the key balance check: the ZFW CG is what you verify against the envelope.
Takeoff Weight (TOW)
Weight at the start of the takeoff run.
Maximum Ramp Weight
Design maximum weight on the ground. Includes a start-up and taxi fuel allowance, which must be used before takeoff.
Maximum Landing Weight
Design maximum weight for landing at the maximum rate of descent.
Fuselage Station (FS)
Longitudinal coordinate in inches, used to locate components and the complete aircraft CG.
Manufacturer's Weight Empty
Dry, clean and empty, except for fluids in closed systems (e.g. hydraulic).
Empty Weight
Manufacturer's weight empty + standard operational items (unusable fuel and oils). The starting point for all loading calculations.
Basic Weight
Empty Weight + crew. The minimum allowable zero fuel weight configuration.
Operating Weight
Empty Weight + all operating items. Equal to TOW less usable fuel, payload and any item expended in flight.
Operating Items
Items needed for the mission: crew, oil, unusable fuel (already in Empty Weight), crew baggage, food, beverages, water, emergency equipment and other items not in Basic Weight.

Mean Aerodynamic Chord (%MAC)

The CG can be expressed as a fuselage station (inches) or as a percentage of MAC. G550 conversion:

%MAC = (CGin − 524.74) ÷ 1.7119
CGin = 524.74 + 1.7119 × %MAC
Reading the formula: LEMAC (leading edge of MAC) = FS 524.74; MAC length = 171.19 in (1% = 1.7119 in). TEMAC ≈ FS 695.93.

Manual example: FS 573.4 → (573.4 − 524.74) ÷ 1.7119 = 28.4 %MAC.

Tip-back situation

The risk of the aircraft tipping back when its CG moves aft of the main landing gear (or wing jack stands). It won't happen on a properly completed aircraft, but it can happen during completion if the ferry ballast is removed without precautions.

Precautions: tie down the forward portion or support the aft portion. Two tie-down methods: (1) the nose gear tie-down fittings (prevents nose gear retraction during completion), or (2) a strap around the forward fuselage, the method Gulfstream uses and recommends.

For the operator, tip-back risk develops only if weight distribution changes after delivery:

  • considerable equipment removed from the forward portion of a defueled aircraft
  • cargo loaded in the baggage compartment
  • personnel working in the aft equipment bay or on the stabilizer
  • aircraft parked nose-up on an inclined surface
  • large accumulation of snow/ice on the stabilizer
NOTE: inflating the main landing gear struts moves the wheels forward, making tip-back more likely. Check the aircraft CG before inflating the main gear struts.

Zero Fuel Weight CG Envelope

If the ZFW CG is within the envelope, the fueled aircraft CG will be within FAA-approved limits for all fuel loadings. The other weight limits (ramp, TOW, LW) still apply.

5001 & sub: full envelope. With ASC 009: below the dashed line only (53,500 lb). Vertices read from the chart: 34.5%/48,250 · 34.5%/54,000 · 34.8%/54,500 · 37.3%/54,500 · 45%/~49,600 · 45%/44,000 · 35.5%/45,700.

ZFW CG Limit Chart — how to use it

  1. Take the current empty weight and moment from the Empty Weight and CG Change Log and enter them on the Flight Loading Computation Form.
  2. Enter the weight and moment of each additional item that is part of the ZFW.
  3. Add them up: total ZFW and total moment.
  4. On the Limit Chart, use the correct area for the ASC status (the shaded area is not valid with ASC 009).
  5. In the left-hand column, find the ZFW line.
  6. Divide the moment by 10,000. If it is less than the lowest or more than the highest value on that line → out of limits: change the loading.
  7. If it is on the line, go straight up to the top of the chart to read the CG in %MAC and as an FS.

Alternate method: Moment ÷ ZFW = CG (in) → FS vs %MAC table → check on the envelope chart.

Fuel

Fuel CG (ground static attitude) comes from the Fuel CG Chart: add fuel weight and moment to the ZFW to get the ramp weight and its CG. Fuel CG moves forward up to ~9,000 lb (FS 526.0), then aft to FS 559.2 at 41,300 lb.

Manual example: ZFW 53,000 lb / 3,116.8 + Fuel 22,000 lb / 1,183.8 = Ramp 75,000 lb / 4,300.6 → CG = 4,300.6 × 10,000 ÷ 75,000 = 573.4 in = 28.4 %MAC.
