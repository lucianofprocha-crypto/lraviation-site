Deciding whether to uplift extra fuel based on the carriage cost factor

What is fuel tankering?

Fuel tankering is the decision to uplift more fuel than the flight requires, taking advantage of a lower price at the departure airport to avoid (or reduce) refueling at the destination, where fuel is more expensive.

The catch is that extra fuel is weight, and carrying that weight makes the aircraft burn more fuel en route - so part of the price advantage is consumed just "carrying" the extra fuel. The longer the flight and the faster the cruise (higher Mach), the bigger this penalty, because the surplus is carried for longer at a higher burn.

savings per gallon = destination price - (origin price x tankering factor)

The tankering factor represents how much fuel is burned to carry each extra gallon over that specific flight - it is always greater than 1, and grows with flight time and cruise Mach.

Worked example (Mach 0.85, 6-hour flight):
Price at DEP: US$ 4.00/gal  |  Price at ARR: US$ 4.90/gal  |  Tankering factor: 1.251

Savings per gallon = 4.90 - (4.00 x 1.251) = 4.90 - 5.004 = -US$ 0.10 per gallon

Even with fuel US$ 0.90 cheaper at origin, on this 6-hour flight at Mach 0.85 tankering does not pay off - the penalty of carrying the extra weight (25.1% burn surcharge) beats the price advantage. If the same flight took only 3 hours (factor 1.090), the math would change to 4.90 - (4.00x1.090) = +US$ 0.54/gallon - then it would pay to uplift as much as possible.
